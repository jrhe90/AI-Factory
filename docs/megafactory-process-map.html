Station flow by line

A station holds one unit for its nominal cycle; the sim varies manual work (CV 15 %) more than automatic work (CV 3 %). Dashed boxes are process sections where the part rides a conveyor, and their time is the dwell (length ÷ speed). Paint times such as 12 / 9 are frame / door kit. Diamonds are test gates: a failed module or pack is re-tested in place at 60 % of the cycle; a paint reject goes to repair. Circled letters connect lanes.

What to check

How the flow behaves

  • Pack line sets the pace. End-of-Line Test is the slowest station at 59 min. With cycle variation, 6 % re-tests, transfers and breakdowns, output settles near 67 min per pack (64–72 across seeds), about 21.5 packs a day.
  • Paint runs ahead. Each pack needs two carriers, a frame and a door kit. The booths pass one carrier every 12 min, so the line can coat about one pack's worth every 24 min. Load & Hang alternates frames and kits so neither runs out at the pack line.
  • Rejects cost a lap. About 5 % of carriers fail inspection. 70 % of those are spot-repaired and rejoin the buffer; the rest are stripped and go round the whole line again, ahead of new work.
  • Modules run ahead too. Four trays take about 44 min of module-line work, so the racks fill to 56 and the module line waits. Module Install is starved only if the racks hold fewer than 4 trays.
  • Breakdowns. Stations fault about once every 40 hours of run time, about 45 min to repair. A booth faults about once every 30 hours and stops its whole conveyor section, which backs up the rail behind it.
  • Exit is gated by the yard. The crane sets a pack down only when the pickup pad is free, and the pad clears only when a carrier pair is free and the lot has a slot.

Open questions

  1. Re-tests and spot repairs always succeed. Do we want a scrap or teardown outcome for repeat failures?
  2. One door kit per pack rides a single carrier. Is that the right split, or do doors and panels need more carriers per pack?
  3. The shop runs one colour, so there are no colour changes or booth cleanouts. Should planned booth cleaning come out of paint capacity?
  4. Rails, cells and door kits arrive without limit. Should inbound supply and the dock be modelled as a constraint?
  5. Shifts run around the clock with no breaks or changeovers. Should planned downtime come out of the 30.6 GWh figure?
